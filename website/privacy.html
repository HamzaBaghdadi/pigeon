Privacy policy

Effective November 17, 2026

The short version

  • Pigeon is an email app that runs on your computer. Your email is stored on your computer and sent only between your computer and your email provider.
  • We don't run servers that receive your email, passwords or Google data. We don't have accounts, analytics, ads or tracking.
  • We never sell or share your data, and we never use it to train AI models.

1. Who we are

Pigeon is a free, open-source email client for Windows, developed by Hamza Baghdadi ("we", "us"). Its source code is public at github.com/HamzaBaghdadi/pigeon, so anyone can check that it does what this policy says. This policy covers the Pigeon desktop app and this website.

2. Data Pigeon handles

To work as your email app, Pigeon handles the following on your computer:

  • Account details: your email address, the account name you choose, and your mail server settings.
  • Sign-in credentials: either a password or app password you type in, or, if you use Sign in with Google, a sign-in token issued by Google. Pigeon never sees your Google password.
  • Your email: messages, attachments, folders and labels, and the read, starred and similar flags, downloaded from your email provider so you can read and search them.
  • Messages you write: drafts and email you send, which Pigeon delivers through your provider's outgoing mail server.
  • Preferences: settings such as theme, notifications and your display name.

We, the developers, do not receive any of this. Pigeon has no Pigeon account, no sign-up and no usage tracking.

How Google user data is used

Pigeon uses Google user data only to provide the email features you see in the app: showing, searching, organizing and sending your email. It is used on your computer only, for you, and at your request.

  • Google user data travels directly between your computer and Google. It does not pass through, and is not stored on, any server operated by us.
  • We do not sell Google user data, transfer it to anyone, or use it for advertising, including personalized or retargeted ads.
  • We do not use it to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
  • No person reads your data. We have no access to it.
  • We do not use Google user data to develop, improve or train generalized AI or machine-learning models.

Pigeon's use and transfer of information received from Google APIs to any other app will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements.

How Google user data is stored and protected

  • Your Google sign-in token is stored in Windows Credential Manager, the operating system's protected credential store, never in Pigeon's own files.
  • Your email is stored in a database file in your Windows user profile, on your own computer, so you can read it offline and search it quickly.
  • All connections to Google are encrypted with TLS.

Removing Google access

Removing your Google account in Pigeon (Settings → Accounts → Remove) deletes its email from your computer and deletes its sign-in token from Windows Credential Manager. You can also revoke Pigeon's access at any time on your Google account permissions page. Nothing in your Gmail account is deleted either way.

4. Where data is stored

  • Email and account details: a local database in your Windows user profile (%AppData%\Pigeon).
  • Passwords and sign-in tokens: Windows Credential Manager.
  • Settings: a settings file in the same folder.
  • Attachments you save: wherever you choose to save them.

This data is under your control. Anyone with access to your Windows account could read it, so keep your computer protected with a password.

5. Connections Pigeon makes

Pigeon only connects to:

  • Your email providers' mail servers (for example Gmail, iCloud or Yahoo), to receive and send your email.
  • Google's sign-in service, only when you use Sign in with Google, to sign in and to renew access.
  • GitHub, a few times a day, to check for Pigeon updates and download them. This request contains no personal data, though GitHub can see your IP address, as with any web request. You can turn update checks off in Settings.
  • Remote images in emails, only when you choose to show them. They're blocked by default because senders can use them to see when and where you opened their email.

Your use of those services is covered by their own privacy policies, such as Google's and GitHub's.

6. Sharing

We don't share your data with anyone, because we never receive it. Pigeon contains no analytics, advertising or third-party tracking code.

7. Keeping and deleting data

  • Pigeon keeps a local copy of an account's email for as long as the account is in Pigeon.
  • Removing an account deletes its local email and its stored password or sign-in token.
  • Uninstalling Pigeon removes the app. To remove everything, also delete the %AppData%\Pigeon folder and any Pigeon entries in Windows Credential Manager.
  • Your email on your provider's servers is never deleted by removing an account or uninstalling Pigeon.

8. Security

Pigeon is designed to keep your email safe: connections are encrypted, credentials are kept in Windows Credential Manager, emails are shown in a locked-down viewer where scripts can't run, remote images are blocked by default, and updates are only installed if they carry the project's digital signature. If you find a security problem, please report it privately through GitHub's security reporting.

9. Children

Pigeon is a general-purpose email app and isn't directed at children under 13. We don't knowingly collect data from anyone, including children.

10. Changes

If this policy changes, we'll update it on this page and change the date at the top. For important changes, we'll also mention them in the release notes on GitHub.

11. Contact

Questions about privacy? Open an issue at github.com/HamzaBaghdadi/pigeon/issues. For anything you'd rather not post publicly, use GitHub's private security reporting.
